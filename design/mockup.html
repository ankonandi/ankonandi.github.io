    TeachOS in seven problems

    Each one started from something I found in interviews or data, not a feature request. The product grew in the order instructors needed it.

    Why TeachOS exists

    In 2025 NxtWave grew to 18 universities. The quality of teaching had to stay at the same level on every campus, and that takes an audit layer. Auditing by people is expensive, because someone has to sit through every class.

    So we built a tool that audits classes for us, and it solved two problems at once:

    • Instructors get feedback on every session and improve.
    • Leadership sees what is happening in every class, end to end, and quality stays consistent across sessions.

    Classroom audio couldn't be trusted

    Completion 84% to 98%

    The problem

    Recordings were lost on reloads, tab switches and network drops. Most instructors taught on laptop mics, and some re-used old recordings to get a report.

    What I did

    • Drove a rebuild into an offline-safe flow with auto-resume, single-tab locking and crash recovery.
    • Added mic detection, external-mic enforcement with fallback, and checks on recording metadata.
    • Ran root-cause analyses to find where recordings were still being lost.

    Result

    83.6% to 97.9% evaluation completion.
    Auto-resume rescued 3,903 recordings in four months.
    External mic use rose from 8% to 31.7%, and sessions flagged as critically low quality fell from 17.1% to 3.0%.

    Reports were an audit nobody used

    Open rate 61% to 85%

    The problem

    Reports came as Word files with long lists of actions. Interviews showed instructors felt judged, not coached.

    What I did

    • Redesigned the report around one decision per session: a Session Snapshot with the top three improvement areas.
    • Gave each area an Action Playbook, plus deltas against the previous session and timestamped audio.

    Result

    Report open rate went from 60.6% to 84.8% after the Snapshot launched.
    Reports opened per month rose from 9,707 to 21,224, with open rate still 78.7% at more than twice the volume.

    Instructors walked into class unprepared

    Demo lectures 1.5% to 40%

    The problem

    Instructors prepped minutes before class with only a slide link.

    What I did

    • Built the preparation layer: a pre-lecture readiness gate, an AI Assessment that quizzes the instructor by voice, demo lectures, and a misconceptions tab.
    • Gave managers visibility of preparation in Team Performance.

    Result

    Lectures with a demo went from 1.5% to 40.1%.
    The same instructor scores +0.20 out of 10 when prepared, and 67% of 303 instructors scored higher.

    Managers coached from memory

    1:1s a month 56 to 985

    The problem

    17 managers coach 500+ instructors, with informal 1:1s and no shared history.

    What I did

    • Designed and built Manager Connect myself in Replit, end to end. It is a separate product that I still own, code, database and maintenance, and it appears inside TeachOS as an embedded page.
    • Shaped Team Performance and manager scorecards around it.

    Result

    3,009 1:1s completed for 520 instructors by 17 managers in six months.
    Monthly 1:1s grew from 56 to 985, and transcript readiness from 40% to 86%.

    Attendance ate teaching time

    ~10 min to ~2 min

    The problem

    Multi-round photo attendance took a large slice of every class, and instructor issues had no tracked channel.

    What I did

    • Led QR attendance from pilot to rollout on four campuses, working with campus operations on devices, network and policy.
    • Built Zoho ticketing that passes session context automatically.

    Result

    Attendance went from about 10 minutes on paper, to about 5 with photos, to about 2 with QR, and it is more authentic and harder to fake.
    Issues raised with full context grew from 19 to 2,144 a month.

    Missing recordings were blamed on Wi-Fi

    25 instructors, 45% of gaps

    The problem

    15.7% of offline lectures had no recording, and the default explanation was connectivity.

    What I did

    • Ran the root-cause analysis on the data myself and set product, operational and escalation actions.

    Result

    The gap was behavioural, not network: most were recordings that never started, and just 25 instructors accounted for 45% of missed lecture recordings. The fix moved from infrastructure to product and compliance.

    Manager Connect up close

    A separate product I built and own. It runs the weekly 1:1 between a Capability Manager and each instructor, from booking to action items, and opens from inside TeachOS with no separate login.

    1. Open slotsmanager drags a duration onto the calendar, or duplicates last week
    2. Bookinstructor requests one slot a week
    3. Approvemanager approves or rejects, not within 30 minutes of the start
    4. Confirmboth get an email with a calendar invite
    5. Meet linkadded by the manager, unlocks 30 minutes before
    6. Close outcompleted or missed, with action items for both sides

    Rules built into the flow

    • Slots can't be created in the past, and only open slots can be edited.
    • A booked session is cancelled from its own page, never from the slot list.
    • A session happening today can't be rescheduled in the app.
    • Reminders go out shortly before a session and only once.
    • Action items become read-only once saved.

    What each person sees

    • Managers: a weekly dashboard with total, completed, scheduled, not booked and missed, a completion rate, pending approvals and today's sessions, plus a colour-coded team view and each instructor's history.
    • Instructors: a status card for the week, their counts, full session history and the action items from every completed 1:1.

    How I work

    Five habits that show up in every project above.

    • Start from a problem in the dataInterviews at three campuses, a behavioural segmentation of instructors, and analysis in Metabase and BigQuery with Claude-assisted queries. A feature request is a hypothesis, not a spec.
    • Build when it's faster than explainingManager Connect in Replit, a video POC, about 20 Lovable prototypes (10 to 15 deployed) and about 10 interactive HTML prototypes. Engineering time goes only into directions that are already validated.
    • Pair every feature with two metricsA leading and a lagging one. Adoption, engagement, behaviour and outcome stay separate so a win isn't overstated.
    • Reliability before noveltyEvaluation quality comes first: subject-specific rubrics, a gold-standard LLM evaluation set, and model migrations as models were deprecated, while keeping cost per evaluation efficient.
    • Decide once, write it downScope, formats and policies are decided, documented and not re-litigated. Every task carries points, status and a written description, and every release goes into release notes.
